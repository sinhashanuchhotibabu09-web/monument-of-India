Featured Monuments

Taj Mahal

The Taj Mahal in Agra is one of the Seven Wonders of the World. Built by Shah Jahan in memory of Mumtaz Mahal, this white marble masterpiece represents eternal love and Mughal architecture.

Qutub Minar

Qutub Minar in Delhi is the tallest brick minaret in the world. It was built in 1193 and showcases Indo-Islamic architecture with detailed carvings and inscriptions.

Gateway of India

Located in Mumbai, the Gateway of India was built to commemorate the visit of King George V and Queen Mary in 1911. It is one of the most famous landmarks in India.

Sun Temple Konark

The Sun Temple in Konark, Odisha, is shaped like a giant chariot. It is famous for its detailed stone carvings and historical significance.

Amer Fort

Amer Fort, also known as Amber Fort, is a majestic 16th-century fortress-palace located atop the Aravalli hills near Jaipur, Rajasthan, and recognized globally as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Constructed primarily out of yellow and pink sandstone blended with white marble, the fort was commissioned by Raja Man Singh I in 1592 as the opulent principal residence of the Rajput Kachwaha rulers. It is celebrated for its stunning architectural fusion of traditional Hindu motifs and grand Mughal aesthetics, featuring large courtyards, frescoed arches, and intricate stone carvings. The sprawling complex is divided into four main functional sections, highlighted by the iconic Ganesh Pol gateway, the vast Diwan-i-Aam (Hall of Public Audience), and the breathtaking Sheesh Mahal (Mirror Palace), whose walls are adorned with thousands of imported concave mirrors that shimmer brilliantly under candlelight. Overlooking the serene waters of Maota Lake—which beautifully mirrors the fort's massive ramparts—Amer Fort remains one of India's most visited cultural landmarks, encapsulating centuries of royal history, artistic mastery, and military strategy.
